04Development Process

The process is boring.
Deliberately.

Five phases, each ending in something you can see, test, or veto. Surprises belong in your market — not in your delivery schedule.

  1. Phase 01

    Discover

    Two weeks inside your problem — users, data, constraints, failure modes. You get an architecture and a fixed-scope plan priced to the line item. If we're not the right fit, this is where we say so.

    → Architecture & fixed-scope plan

  2. Phase 02

    Architect

    System design, data models, and interface foundations settled before feature code begins. Every consequential decision is written down with its trade-offs — future engineers inherit reasons, not folklore.

    → Technical spec & design system

  3. Phase 03

    Build

    Working software from the first week, demonstrated every week, in repositories you own from day one. Progress is something you can use — not a percentage in a status deck.

    → Working software, every week

  4. Phase 04

    Harden

    Load tests, security review, failure drills, observability. We try to break it before your customers get the chance — and we launch only when we can't.

    → A launch you can sleep through

  5. Phase 05

    Evolve

    After launch, the same engineers stay on — measured against the numbers that justified the project, shipping the next capability instead of a maintenance invoice.

    → Compounding improvement

09FAQ

Fair questions,
straight answers.

If yours isn't here, ask a partner directly — you'll have an answer within one business day.

Who do you typically work with?

Funded startups shipping their first serious product, scale-ups mid-rearchitecture, and enterprise teams accountable for systems that cannot fail — most often in fintech, healthcare, logistics, and AI-native software. We run a small number of engagements at once, and we protect that limit because it is why the work holds up.

How do engagements work?

Every engagement starts with a two-week Discovery sprint that produces an architecture, a delivery plan, and a fixed-scope proposal with a price attached. That plan is yours to keep — take it to any builder you like. Most clients continue with us, but the decision should be easy either way.

Do you build production AI or prototypes?

Production systems. Prototypes are how we test assumptions in week two, not what we hand over in week twelve. Every AI system we ship includes an evaluation suite, monitoring, guardrails, and a documented cost model — because a model that cannot be measured cannot be trusted.

Who will actually work on our project?

The people you meet in the first call. There is no bench of juniors behind a senior pitch — our average engineer has eleven years of experience, and no one splits their week across multiple clients.

Who owns the code and IP?

You do, from the first commit. We build in your repositories and your cloud accounts, so ownership is never a handover at the end — it is the default from day one. If we vanished mid-project, you would lose nothing but us.

What happens after launch?

A stabilization period and a documented handover are built into every engagement, so your team can run what we built. Most clients then keep a senior team on retainer for new capabilities — 98% of our business is companies that have shipped with us before.
